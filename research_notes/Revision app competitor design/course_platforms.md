# Structured A level learning platforms: Seneca Learning, Up Learn, Isaac Science (Isaac Physics / Isaac Maths)

Method note (read first): the egress proxy in this session refused direct page fetches for isaacphysics.org, isaacscience.org, help.senecalearning.com and help.uplearn.co.uk, so every finding below comes from search-engine snippets and summaries of those pages (titles and URLs as returned by search), not from a full reading of each page. Treat exact wording as close paraphrase, and check UI details against the live sites before copying them. Research date: 22 Oct 2026. Undated items are flagged where the source was visibly old.

## 1. Onboarding and account sign-up (fields, social login, school/teacher linking, under-18s, gating)

### Takeaway
All three use the same basic pattern: email or Google/Microsoft single sign-on, then a role choice (student, teacher or parent), then joining a class with a teacher code. Isaac asks students to confirm they are 13 or older, with ages 10 to 12 allowed with a parent's permission. Seneca makes you pick a school and year group and gives you an optional parent-email link. Up Learn starts each course with a diagnostic of the whole syllabus. Only Seneca and Up Learn put features behind a paywall.

### Cited Findings
**Seneca**
- Sign-up order: personal details, agree to the terms, choose Student, Teacher or Parent, pick your school and year group, optionally enter a parent's email to link a parent account, then "add courses and you can start studying". — [Seneca help: How to sign up as a student](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/3661264-how-to-sign-up-to-seneca-as-a-student)
- Google and Microsoft single sign-on are offered as an alternative to email. — [Seneca help: sign up](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/3661264-how-to-sign-up-to-seneca-as-a-student)
- To join a school class, the teacher sends a class code or an invite. Schools also create pupil accounts in bulk. — [Seneca help: sign up](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/3661264-how-to-sign-up-to-seneca-as-a-student)
- Age handling: by creating an account you confirm you are over 13 or have a parent's permission. No separate under-13 flow was found. (The terms page was about 2 years old when indexed.) — [Seneca T&Cs](https://senecalearning.com/en-gb/terms-and-conditions/)
- Courses are organised by exam board ("1500+ exam board specific A Level, GCSE & KS3 courses"). No onboarding diagnostic or placement test was found. — [Seneca home](https://senecalearning.com/en-gb/)
- Free vs Premium: the free courses stay fully usable. Premium adds "800+ Premium courses, Premium learning modes, AI-marked exam questions, Magic quiz assignments and Amelia AI Assistant". Premium is UK-only. A school-bought "sync" package upgrades student accounts automatically, and status is shown under Settings > Premium tab. — [Seneca help: What is Premium](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/2663301-what-is-premium); [Seneca help: can't access school premium](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/5554463-i-can-t-access-school-premium)
- Tiers include "Premium" (AI-marked exam questions) and "Premium AI+" (Amelia plus unlimited exam-question practice). — [Seneca International Schools](https://senecalearning.com/en-gb/international-schools/)
- One iOS reviewer says Premium costs "at least £15 a month". This is a single review, not an official price. — [Seneca App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/seneca/id1631302657)

**Isaac Science (formerly Isaac Physics)**
- Rebrand: Isaac Physics is now called **Isaac Science** and lives on isaacscience.org. It brings Isaac Physics, Chemistry, Biology and Maths together in one place, and existing accounts moved over. I found no exact launch date. — [isaacphysics.org/pages/isaacscience](https://isaacphysics.org/pages/isaacscience); [New look Isaac Physics](https://isaacscience.org/pages/isaacscience_coming_soon)
- Registration: email, or log in with a Google or Microsoft account. A checkbox asks you to confirm "you are at least 13 years old, or aged 10-12 and have asked for permission from a parent or carer". — [Isaac Student FAQs](https://isaacscience.org/support/student); [Register](https://isaacscience.org/register)
- Teacher linking: the teacher gives you a code or link. On the homepage, students enter it in the **"Join a group"** box and press Connect, then explicitly agree to let the teacher see their data. The same code also works in **My account > "Teacher Connections"**. The teacher can see progress on any assignments set in that group. — [Isaac Student FAQs](https://isaacscience.org/support/student)
- Completely free (run by the University of Cambridge). — [Mumsnet teacher thread](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/the_staffroom/5568241-homework-for-a-level-science); [Cambridge Impact Map](https://impactmap.cam.ac.uk/isaac-online-platforms-improving-a-level-grades-for-physics-and-computer-science/)

**Up Learn**
- Each course starts with a **diagnostic** that maps the student against the full syllabus. — [Up Learn Biology page](https://uplearn.co.uk/biology)
- A help article "How do I begin my Up Learn journey?" exists, but its contents could not be read. — [Up Learn help](https://help.uplearn.co.uk/en/articles/3746108-what-s-the-best-way-to-use-up-learn)
- Exam boards: aims to cover AQA, Edexcel and OCR, and "some subjects also support WJEC, **CCEA**, Cambridge International and IB". Coverage differs by subject page. — [Up Learn FAQ](https://uplearn.co.uk/faq)
- Paid: the comparison page lists "£319.99 until 2027 exams" per subject. What you get depends on the plan: video lessons, quiz-check questions, video quizzes, exam how-to videos, summary videos, exam practice questions, progress quizzes, exclusive papers and 24/7 tutor support. — [Up Learn landing](https://get.uplearn.co.uk/home-a-level/); [Up Learn FAQ](https://uplearn.co.uk/faq)
- Money-back guarantee if you don't get at least an A at A level. You must complete the course and meet the eligibility criteria (a reviewer says that means a 90% Up Score). A company representative said about 20 refunds were paid out of roughly 1,200 eligible students in one year (the Mumsnet post is undated and probably old). — [Up Learn landing](https://get.uplearn.co.uk/home-a-level/); [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/uplearn.co.uk); [Mumsnet](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/further_education/3761940-any-experience-with-an-uplearn-subscription-to-help-with-a-levels)
- Parent account: track progress by topic, including Up Score, and receive weekly progress emails. — [Up Learn FAQ](https://uplearn.co.uk/faq)
- Schools are onboarded through Wonde (MIS sync) and get a school dashboard. — [Wonde: Up Learn](https://www.wonde.com/uk/application/up-learn); [Introducing the School Dashboard](https://uplearn.co.uk/resource-articles/news-introducing-school-dashboard)

### Inferences
- A minimal free-app sign-up that matches the market: Google/Microsoft SSO plus email, a single age checkbox ("13+, or 10–12 with parent permission", copying Isaac), then pick board and subjects. A CCEA app can skip the board choice entirely, which saves a step.
- Isaac's explicit "allow teacher to see my data" consent on joining a group is a cheap pattern worth copying for GDPR/ICO Children's Code alignment.
- Up Learn's syllabus-wide diagnostic is the only real placement test among the three. A short per-unit diagnostic quiz is an easy way to get a student to useful learning quickly.

### Gaps
- Exact Seneca and Up Learn screen order, how long it takes to reach the first lesson, and whether Up Learn has a free trial: the help pages could not be fetched and search snippets did not cover them.
- Current official Seneca Premium prices for 2026.
- Isaac's date-of-birth handling beyond the checkbox (for example, whether DOB is collected).

## 2. Course/topic navigation and the learning loop

### Takeaway
Seneca: short sections of bite-sized content cards interleaved with questions, plus adaptive "Smart Learning", "Wrong Answers" mode and a per-topic "memory strength" score. Up Learn: a linear course of short video lessons, each gated by quizzes ("quiz-check" questions before videos let strong students skip). Isaac: not a course at all but a problem bank ("learning by doing"), with concept pages, topic question decks and teacher-set "gameboards".

### Cited Findings
- Seneca Smart Learning Mode "tells you what to learn and when to maximise your progress". Once a course has enough data, its algorithm recommends which sections to study. — [Seneca help: What is Premium](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/2663301-what-is-premium)
- Seneca Wrong Answers Mode: go back to a section you have studied and practise only the answers you got wrong. — [Seneca help: What is Premium](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/2663301-what-is-premium); [Seneca help: wrong answers count](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/3950895-the-number-of-wrong-answers-shown-isn-t-what-i-expected)
- Seneca lessons are built from individual steps with knowledge checks along the way: gap-fills, scrolling bullet points and "short, sharp bursts of exam questions". — [Nottingham Girls' Academy guide](https://www.nottinghamgirlsacademy.org/_site/data/files/documents/homelearning/2070092B5F467405406E2E0D1316C5F1.pdf); [John Roan student guide](https://www.thejohnroanschool.org.uk/Portals/0/adam/AccordionTwo/Avp8IZiYzkebJnlxL1pORQ/Content/User%20Guide%20-%20Student.pdf)
- Each Seneca topic has a **memory strength** score, a long-term measure that rises when you revisit topics at intervals. Cramming in one sitting is discouraged. Seneca says its algorithm draws on visual imagery, interleaving and spacing. Students earn XP and rewards. — [Cotham School guide](https://www.cotham.bristol.sch.uk/_site/data/files/curriculum/6F6A3B3EA4FF52661F7E7F95BCC08CFA.pdf); [Gloucester Academy](https://www.gloucesteracademy.co.uk/students/homework-and-revision-guidance/seneca-learning); [Common Sense Education review](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/seneca-learning)
- One reviewer says Seneca "cram mode" was recently limited to 20 questions unless you upgrade. — [Google Play listing reviews](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.senecalearning.app&hl=en)
- Up Learn Maths: "short, focused lessons" where "each lesson builds on the last", with quizzes between sessions to confirm understanding before moving on, and a "real-time progress tracker". — [Up Learn Maths](https://uplearn.co.uk/maths)
- Up Learn: "all of our courses include either section quizzes or quiz-check questions before the videos". A reviewer says students who already know a topic can tick activities off without watching every video. — [Trustpilot company reply](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/uplearn.co.uk?page=4)
- Up Learn suggests 6–10 hours per week per subject: 16–25 weeks for Physics, and up to 35 weeks for Maths. Time only counts once a video or quiz is fully completed. — [Up Learn help: how long](https://help.uplearn.co.uk/en/articles/5172216-how-long-will-it-take-me-to-complete-the-course); [Up Learn help: time tracked](https://help.uplearn.co.uk/en/articles/12469616-how-is-my-time-on-up-learn-tracked)
- Up Learn Biology advertises "800+ practice questions, examiner-reviewed papers". — [Up Learn Biology](https://uplearn.co.uk/biology)
- Isaac's philosophy, from co-founder Lisa Jardine-Wright, is "learning by doing", built on many problems rather than a course. Questions come with concept sheets, graded hints and hint videos. — [Cambridge news](https://www.cam.ac.uk/news/website-helps-maths-and-physics-school-students-work-it-out); [Isaac YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/user/isaacphysics)
- Isaac navigation: subject hubs (/physics, /physics/a_level), a **Question Finder** that filters by stage, topic and difficulty, and ready-made **question decks by topic**. The decks are "not comprehensive". — [Isaac A Level Physics](https://isaacscience.org/physics/a_level); [Question decks](https://isaacscience.org/physics/a_level/question_decks)
- Isaac platform development is done by Cambridge's Department of Computer Science and Technology. Isaac recorded its 200 millionth question attempt (2025/26 story). — [Cambridge: 200 millionth](https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/cambridge-isaac-science-200-millionth-question); [CST news](https://www.cst.cam.ac.uk/news/200-millionth-question-attempt-made-learning-platform-co-developed-here)

### Inferences
- For a free CCEA app, a Seneca-like loop (a short explanation card, then a quick check, then spaced re-surfacing driven by a per-topic memory score) plus an Isaac-like problem bank filtered by unit/topic/difficulty would cover both "learn" and "practise". Up Learn's "quiz before video to skip" pattern works well for fast learners, and its absence is the slowness that reviewers complain about.

### Gaps
- No primary source found on Seneca or Up Learn A level Maths and Physics lesson granularity (number of sections, cards per section).
- No detail on how Isaac gameboards are presented to students. The research paper on gameboard design could not be located.

## 3. Exam questions: structure, progress tracking, mastery, streaks, difficulty levels

### Takeaway
Isaac has the clearest difficulty model: every question is either Practice (P1–P3, one concept) or Challenge (C1–C3, several concepts or more creativity), and the level is relative to stage. Up Learn uses a single "Up Score" mastery metric and section scores that turn green. Seneca uses memory strength plus AI-marked, board-specific 1–9 mark exam questions. None of them is organised paper by paper. All are topic-first. No confirmed streak mechanic was found for any of the three.

### Cited Findings
- Isaac: Practice questions "require one concept or equation". Challenge questions require "one or more concepts, or more creativity". In December 2021 the old Levels 1–6 were replaced by P1–P3 and C1–C3. The same question can be Practice for an A level student and Challenge for a GCSE student. — [Isaac Dec 2021 update](https://isaacscience.org/pages/update_dec_2021); [Isaac student FAQs](https://isaacscience.org/support/student/questions)
- Isaac "My progress" shows total question parts attempted this academic year. A student could not separate out challenge parts, which are the ones that count for the Senior Physics Challenge (TSR thread, about 2 years old). — [TSR thread](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=7549375)
- Up Learn **Up Score** "shows how much of the course you've mastered". It combines content completed, quiz performance and exam-paper results, and is shown for the whole course and for each module. A help page explains how to get a **section score to green**. — [Up Learn help: Up Score](https://help.uplearn.co.uk/en/articles/10517296-how-do-i-increase-my-up-score); [Up Learn help: section green](https://help.uplearn.co.uk/en/articles/7669082-how-do-i-improve-my-score-for-a-section); [Up Learn help: track effort](https://help.uplearn.co.uk/en/articles/10517171-how-do-i-track-my-effort-on-up-learn)
- Seneca exam questions are exam-board specific, worth 1 to 9 marks, and there are 10,000+ open-ended ones. They are marked by an LLM with human oversight, and teachers receive the reports. — [Seneca help: Analyse my answer](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/13458864-analyse-my-answer); [Seneca CPD: open-ended questions](https://senecalearning.com/en-GB/revision-notes/teacher-cpd/cpd-applications-of-artificial-intelligence-in-education/1-2-6-open-ended-questions)
- Students can flag an AI mark as inaccurate, and teachers can re-mark it. — [Seneca help: re-mark](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/8536842-how-do-i-re-mark-ai-marked-exam-questions)
- A Seneca school guide says it is "a good alternative to past papers". — [St Albans school doc](https://www.st-albans.suffolk.sch.uk/_site/data/files/documents/sixth%20form/learning-plus/9C706750F06927D17E6477F38F9F986D.pdf)
- Up Learn reviewer complaint: maths "doesn't have exam style questions". Another review calls the Maths and Physics content "too basic". — [Trustpilot summary](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/uplearn.co.uk?page=5)

### Inferences
- Paper-by-paper navigation (by CCEA unit and series year) is a gap the topic-first competitors leave open. Because CCEA has fewer papers, offering both "by paper" and "by topic" views of the same tagged question bank is feasible.
- Copy Isaac's two-axis difficulty (concept count × level), using honest labels such as "Practice 1–3 / Challenge 1–3".
- A single mastery number that combines coverage, quiz accuracy and exam results (Up Score) is easy to understand, and colour-coded section states (red/amber/green) are an established convention.

### Gaps
- No confirmed streak or daily-goal feature on any of the three. Isaac "My progress" may show streaks, but I found no source confirming it.
- No evidence that any of them offers a full timed mock paper mode for Maths or Physics.

## 4. Worked solutions, hints and video UX

### Takeaway
Isaac deliberately publishes no worked solutions. It offers tiered hints instead (concept clue, then how to organise the information, then a diagram to draw, then equations, with some video hints), and gives instant feedback on significant figures and units. Up Learn's teaching is video-first: short lessons, summary videos and "exam how-to" videos. Seneca explains answers in text and through AI feedback.

### Cited Findings
- Isaac hints sit under each question and give clues about the concepts needed, how to organise the information, what diagram to draw and useful equations. Some questions have video hints. — [Isaac student FAQs](https://isaacscience.org/support/student/questions)
- Isaac does not publish worked solutions. A third-party site says this is deliberate, because the aim is to challenge students to build their own understanding. — [alevelphysicsonline Isaac page](https://www.alevelphysicsonline.com/isaac-science)
- Isaac checks significant figures and warns when an answer has a sig-fig problem. Answers are expected to carry a suitable unit. — [Cambridge-authored excerpt (M. Warner)](https://www.tcm.phy.cam.ac.uk/~mw141/MasteringPhysics-excerpt.pdf)
- Isaac symbolic answers use a graphical equation editor that produces Python-like text, which is then checked. Direct text entry, first a beta, is now available to everyone. — [Isaac Labs: text entry](https://labs.isaacphysics.org/equation-editor-text-entry.html); [Isaac Labs: text entry for all](https://labs.isaacphysics.org/text-entry-no-longer-beta.html); [Raspberry Pi Foundation paper](https://www.raspberrypi.org/app/uploads/2020/04/Effective-use-of-mathematical-equations-in-an-online-learning-environment.pdf)
- Up Learn content types: video lessons, video quizzes, exam how-to videos and summary videos. — [Up Learn FAQ](https://uplearn.co.uk/faq)
- Up Learn connectivity complaint: losing signal forces a page refresh and a rewatch of the video and questions. — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/uplearn.co.uk?page=4)
- Seneca AI feedback criticised as weak, "mostly repeating answers without explanation" (one iOS review). — [Seneca App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/seneca/id1631302657)

### Inferences
- A layered "hint 1, hint 2, ... then full worked solution" reveal sits between Isaac's no-solutions approach and the full model answers typical of revision sites. For self-study revision, offering a step-by-step reveal after hints is likely to suit students better than leaving solutions out.
- Copy Isaac's sig-fig and unit checking for Physics numeric answers. It is a distinctive feature that teaches good habits.
- Save video and question progress locally so a dropped connection doesn't force a rewatch.

### Gaps
- Typical Up Learn video length and whether videos have chapters. Not found.
- Isaac video-hint length and format. Not found.

## 5. AI tutor/chat features

### Takeaway
Up Learn's AI Tutor sits in the messenger (choose "Tutoring"), is grounded in Up Learn's own content, is tailored to the specification, tells students to double-check key information, hands off to a human when the student types "speak to a human", and asks for a rating at the end of each conversation. Seneca's "Amelia" sits behind the Premium paywall. It marks long answers ("Analyse My Answer") and explains why an answer was wrong. Isaac has no AI tutor that I could find.

### Cited Findings
- Up Learn AI Tutor: instant help that "uses the same trusted Up Learn content", is tailored to your A level spec, and can explain concepts, clear up misunderstandings and "guide your thinking step by step". To use it, open the messenger, choose Tutoring and type. It carries the caveat "it's always best to double-check key information". Saying "speak to a human" passes you to a human tutor, and there is a rating at the end of each conversation. — [Up Learn help: What is the AI Tutor](https://help.uplearn.co.uk/en/articles/13390598-what-is-the-ai-tutor); [older version](https://help.uplearn.co.uk/en/articles/10743612-what-is-the-ai-tutor)
- Up Learn says AI-powered courses do most of the teaching. Human tutors are reached through the chat icon, and students are asked to watch the relevant lesson before messaging. — [Up Learn help: tutoring](https://help.uplearn.co.uk/en/articles/13390446-how-does-up-learn-tutoring-work)
- Seneca: after submitting an exam answer, the student clicks **Analyse My Answer** to see what they did well and where they went wrong. They can then ask Amelia why the answer was wrong and how to improve it. In teacher-set assignments the feedback is streamlined, with no chat. — [Seneca help: Analyse my answer](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/13458864-analyse-my-answer); [Seneca help: Amelia](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/6992722-how-do-i-use-amelia-ai-assistant); [Seneca help: Premium AI for Schools](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/12959827-premium-ai-for-schools)
- Seneca AI marking complaints: sometimes it "couldn't mark" an answer and the student had to self-mark (Seneca said the feature was still in development). One reviewer found the questions basic. — [Trustpilot](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/senecalearning.com?page=5)

### Inferences
- Patterns worth copying for a free app's AI help: ground answers in your own notes and spec, show a "double-check" disclaimer, put the AI behind an explicit action on a question ("Explain my mistake") rather than offering open chat, give a thumbs or rating at the end, and always keep a non-AI fallback (worked solution, report button).
- In teacher-assigned (assessment) contexts, turn chat off, as Seneca does, so the AI cannot hand students answers during set work.

### Gaps
- No source on rate limits, model used, or content-safety filters for either AI tutor.

## 6. Report-an-error and feedback flows

### Takeaway
Seneca has an always-visible feedback button in the bottom-left corner. Reports go to the content team, which usually fixes them within one business day. Seneca also lets students flag inaccurate AI marks. No equivalent flow was confirmed for Isaac or Up Learn.

### Cited Findings
- Seneca: content is written by senior examiners, but mistakes slip in. To report one, click the **feedback button in the bottom-left corner**. The comment goes to the content team and is usually fixed within one business day. — [Seneca help: report a mistake](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/2483276-where-can-i-report-a-mistake-in-the-content)
- Seneca also has a help article on "In-App Reporting" and a support email (learnmore@seneca.io) where users can send the question or a screenshot. — [Seneca help: In-App Reporting](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/8264022-in-app-reporting); [Trustpilot replies](https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/senecalearning.com)
- Seneca AI-mark flag: a student can mark the AI marking as inaccurate, and the teacher sees it and can re-mark. — [Seneca help: re-mark](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/8536842-how-do-i-re-mark-ai-marked-exam-questions)
- Up Learn AI Tutor: an end-of-conversation rating. — [Up Learn help](https://help.uplearn.co.uk/en/articles/13390598-what-is-the-ai-tutor)

### Inferences
- For a solo founder, a per-question "Report a problem" control that captures the question ID, the student's answer and a free-text note is the essential feature. Seneca's one-day fix SLA is worth stating publicly to build trust.

### Gaps
- Isaac's in-question feedback/report mechanism. Not confirmed (I believe one exists, but found no source).

## 7. Progress dashboards, mobile vs web, visual style

### Takeaway
Seneca has native iOS and Android apps, playful, animated visuals and XP rewards, though users complain the interface is cluttered. Up Learn is web-first, with student Up Score views, parent weekly emails and a school dashboard. Isaac is a web platform with a "My progress" page and teacher assignment tracking. All three put a lot of effort into teacher and school dashboards.

### Cited Findings
- Seneca apps: Google Play ("Seneca – KS3, GCSE & A Level", about 3.9 stars on one listing) and App Store. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.senecalearning.app&hl=en_IE); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/seneca/id1631302657)
- Seneca clutter complaints: "bombarded by bloat whenever you finish a question", distracting background animations, and slow loading from "silly gifs". — [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/senecalearning.com?page=9)
- Up Learn school dashboard launch. Parents get progress by topic and weekly emails. — [Up Learn news](https://uplearn.co.uk/resource-articles/news-introducing-school-dashboard); [Up Learn FAQ](https://uplearn.co.uk/faq)
- Isaac: teachers set assignments and see progress. Teachers report saving an average of 3.8 hours a week on workload (self-reported, from Cambridge's own impact page). — [Cambridge Impact Map](https://impactmap.cam.ac.uk/isaac-online-platforms-improving-a-level-grades-for-physics-and-computer-science/)

### Inferences
- A calm, low-animation interface is itself a way to stand out from Seneca.

### Gaps
- No dedicated Up Learn or Isaac native app was found. Mobile web is presumably the route, but this is unconfirmed.

## 8. Known user complaints and praise

### Takeaway
Seneca is praised for being free, easy and good for knowledge recall. It is criticised for features moving behind the paywall, login bugs, progress not saving and clutter. Up Learn is praised for clear structure and bite-sized explanations. It is criticised for price (£319.99 per subject), slow or repetitive lessons, and maths content that is too basic and lacks exam-style questions. Isaac is praised by teachers as free, rigorous and good for problem solving. It has a steeper learning curve and no worked solutions.

### Cited Findings
- Seneca review summary: login issues are the most-cited complaint (48 mentions), followed by glitches, progress not saving and repetitive questions. — [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/senecalearning.com?page=9); [mwm.ai aggregator](https://mwm.ai/apps/seneca/1631302657)
- Seneca: Premium "feels like no big difference" from free. Previously free features now require Premium. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.senecalearning.app&hl=en)
- Seneca's rating varies by source: about 3.9 on Google Play vs 2.3★ from 207 reviews on one aggregator. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.senecalearning.app&hl=en); [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/seneca/1631302657)
- Up Learn Trustpilot: around 4.3–4.5 per the pages found. Up Learn's own page claims 4.8 from 2,400+ reviews, which conflicts with the roughly 900 reviews visible. Praise centres on clear structure, bite-sized explanations and interactive quizzes. Complaints: too slow or repetitive, expensive, maths lacks exam-style questions. — [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/uplearn.co.uk); [Up Learn landing](https://get.uplearn.co.uk/home-a-level/)
- Up Learn's "97% of students who finish the course" hit A/A* is a self-reported company claim. — [Up Learn landing](https://get.uplearn.co.uk/home-a-level/)
- A student on TSR: you must put in the time and effort or the guarantee won't refund. — [TSR](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=4523462); see also [TSR: Is Uplearn good for maths?](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=7116720)
