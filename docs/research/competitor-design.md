# Steal eleven competitors' best revision patterns

BCS Academy's biggest design advantage is one that no competitor offers: **nobody serves CCEA A level Maths, Further Maths and Physics natively, and nobody pairs free, account-free access with worked solutions and progress tracking**. Save My Exams lists CCEA A Level Maths past-paper PDFs but shows no CCEA topic questions and no CCEA A level Physics. Physics & Maths Tutor (PMT) has no CCEA A level pages at all. Up Learn says only that "some subjects" support CCEA, and charges £319.99 per subject. The highest-impact patterns a solo founder can copy are cheap ones. The first is PMT's "no login, two taps to a question" path, combined with Duolingo's "use first, sign up to save progress". The second is one tagged question bank shown both paper by paper and topic by topic. The third is a hint ladder that ends in a step-by-step written solution, which merges Khan Academy's per-step hints with Dr Frost Maths' "explanation after every answer". The fourth is Khan's four-state mastery model. The fifth is a "Report a problem" link on every question, as Khan and Duolingo have. The sixth is a low-bar streak with freezes in place of leaderboards. What to avoid is just as clear from competitors' complaints: Save My Exams' billing traps, PMT's video ads and exam-season crashes, Dr Frost's rigid answer-format marking, Seneca's animation clutter and lost progress, and open-ended AI chat that hands out answers. The last of these is harmful: an unguarded AI tutor cut later exam scores by 17% in a large controlled study, and the DfE's January 2026 standards now discourage it. One caveat applies throughout: during this research, direct page fetches to most competitor sites were blocked. Flows and features here come from the companies' own help centres, pricing pages and reviews as indexed by search. **Exact on-screen layouts, colours and click counts were not verified and should be checked live before copying pixel-for-pixel.**

## No competitor builds for CCEA, so the structure itself is a feature

The market splits into two groups. The first is **no-account content libraries** (PMT, Corbettmaths). Here a student goes Subject → Level → Board → resource list and reaches a question in a few clicks without signing in. The second is **account-centric platforms** (Save My Exams, Seneca, Up Learn, Dr Frost, Isaac). These let you browse but push you to sign up before you can practise, track progress or use AI. For a CCEA student, both groups come up short. Save My Exams' CCEA A Level Maths page (exam code A2212) lists Unit 1 Pure and Unit 2 Applied papers by year back to about 2019, but there is no sign of CCEA-specific topic questions, and its CCEA Physics section seems to cover GCSE only ([SME CCEA past papers](https://www.savemyexams.com/a-level/maths/ccea/past-papers/); [SME Physics](https://www.savemyexams.com/subjects/physics/)). PMT's CCEA search results surface only CAIE and WJEC/Eduqas pages. CCEA papers sit on smaller sites, catalogued by unit code: Physics APH11/APH21/APH31/APH32, and Maths SMT11/SMT21/AMT11/AMT21 ([RevisionScience](https://revisionscience.com/a2-level-level-revision/physics-level-revision/physics-level-past-papers/ccea-level-physics-past-papers); [RevisionMaths](https://RevisionMaths.com/level-maths/level-maths-past-papers/ccea-level-maths-past-papers)). Up Learn says "some subjects also support WJEC, CCEA…" ([Up Learn FAQ](https://uplearn.co.uk/faq)), and Dr Frost's free self-marking past papers list "AQA, Edexcel, OCR & more" without confirming CCEA ([DFM Past Papers](https://www.drfrost.org/revision/papers)).

The design implication goes beyond positioning. Because the app serves only one board, it can **skip the board picker that every competitor's onboarding needs**, and it can name things the way CCEA students and teachers already do (AS 1, AS 2, A2 1, A2 2, with codes such as SMT11 and APH11). Use those unit codes as the top level of navigation and in breadcrumbs, for example "Further Maths › A2 1 › Complex numbers". Every competitor uses a strict tree: board/level → subject → module → topic. Dr Frost adds a skill layer underneath ("Key Skills", with expandable topic drop-downs on the left and recommended skills) ([DFM student guide](https://drfrost.org/docs/DFM-GettingStartedForStudents.pdf)). Copy that tree, and on mobile render it as a drill-down list with a sticky breadcrumb, not a left sidebar.

### Why the top three outrank everything else

**Value before sign-up** comes first because forced accounts measurably suppress conversion. A Baymard survey found **19% of shoppers abandoned an order because they did not want to create an account** ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/reduce-cart-abandonment)). Duolingo built its onboarding round delaying registration "until the moment when users must register in order to progress further" ([Appcues GoodUX](https://goodux.appcues.com/blog/duolingo-user-onboarding)). Seneca does the opposite: a six-step sign-up that asks for role, school and year group before any content ([Seneca help](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/3661264-how-to-sign-up-to-seneca-as-a-student)). Login problems are its most-cited complaint, with 48 mentions in one review summary ([Trustpilot Seneca](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/senecalearning.com?page=9)). When the account is offered, copy Isaac's lightest version: Google sign-in, then a single checkbox, "I am at least 13, or 10–12 with a parent's permission" ([Isaac register](https://isaacscience.org/register)). Do not ask for name, school or date of birth, which also keeps BCS Academy aligned with the ICO Children's Code standards on data minimisation and no privacy-weakening nudges ([ICO code](https://ico.org.uk/media/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services-2-1.pdf)).

**The dual-view bank** comes second because it is the clearest gap in the market and it fits CCEA's small paper count. Isaac's question decks are "not comprehensive" and topic-only ([Isaac decks](https://isaacscience.org/physics/a_level/question_decks)). Up Learn reviewers complain that the maths "doesn't have exam style questions" ([Trustpilot Up Learn](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/uplearn.co.uk?page=5)). The learning science supports building both views. Practice testing and distributed practice were the only two techniques Dunlosky et al. rated "high utility" ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)), and retrieval practice works best with a **1–6 day lag before the final test** ([Learning Scientists](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2017/2/9-1)). Topic view supports spaced review early in the year, and paper view supports timed stamina practice in the final weeks.

**Self-marking by mark point** comes third because SME's own tiering shows where the value lies. Mark schemes are free on every tier, but **model answers are unlimited only on Premium** ([SME tiers](https://www.savemyexams.com/learning-hub/support/why-pay-for-save-my-exams/)). A free app that gives every CCEA question a walkthrough at the level of individual mark points turns SME's paywalled asset into BCS Academy's default. Make the student commit an attempt before the reveal so that retrieval still happens. Note one unverified risk: no evidence was found on how accurately students self-mark against mark schemes, and overconfidence is likely, so the four-state mastery model should still require mixed-quiz evidence before awarding "Mastered".

### Worked solutions should be tapped through, not watched

The video-first competitors (Up Learn, DFM, Corbettmaths) depend on studios or years of back catalogue that a solo founder does not have, and the evidence does not favour video anyway. Worked examples give a medium benefit over unguided problem-solving ([Learning Scientists on Barbieri et al. 2023](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2024/1/25-1)). Prompted self-explanation adds **g = 0.55** ([Bisra et al. 2018](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/S10648-018-9456-4)). Segmenting gives small-to-medium gains in retention and transfer ([DOAJ meta-analysis](https://doaj.org/article/d1fdb305193948a5a5bced1627ecef50)). Instructional explanations added to examples, on their own, had effects not significantly different from zero ([Renkl](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Alexander-Renkl/publication/252065774_Learning_from_worked-out_examples_The_transition_from_instructional_explanations_to_self-explanation_prompts/links/0c96053bbcc1ac1622000000/Learning-from-worked-out-examples-The-transition-from-instructional-explanations-to-self-explanation-prompts.pdf)). In other words, telling students is not enough; asking them to explain is what works. The design to build is therefore a static walkthrough with one step per tap. Each step has a one-line reason, a mark-scheme tag (M1, A1), an occasional "Why can we divide by cos x here?" prompt with its own reveal, and a "try the next step yourself" faded mode the second time a student sees it. Copy DFM's rule that an explanation appears after every answer, right or wrong, with no penalty for wrong attempts ([DFM guide](https://drfrost.org/docs/DFM-GettingStartedForStudents.pdf)). Add videos later, only for the highest-traffic questions: one clip per question part, under about six minutes, which is where edX engagement fell off sharply ([Guo et al. 2014](https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf?_blank)). Save watch progress locally, because Up Learn users who lose signal have to refresh the page and rewatch the video ([Trustpilot Up Learn](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/uplearn.co.uk?page=4)).

## Competitors' complaints form a ready-made list of things not to build

The most useful source in this research is the complaint record. Each major complaint maps to a concrete rule. **Save My Exams** draws billing complaints: "they are still taking money from my bank" after cancelling, and trial charges that land if you cancel too late. Its Essential tier is "charged at sign-up" with no trial ([Trustpilot SME](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.savemyexams.com); [SME tiers](https://www.savemyexams.com/learning-hub/support/why-pay-for-save-my-exams/)). The rule for BCS Academy is no metering, no allowance counters and no locked "Premium" chips anywhere in the interface. SME also faces a trust complaint that some notes "are written completely by AI", so BCS Academy should state openly who wrote each solution. **PMT** users call its ads "always videos, which is super distracting" and say the site "crashes and doesn't load" when exams are close ([Trustpilot PMT](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/physicsandmathstutor.com)). The rule is no ads, plus an offline-capable service worker with static hosting so the app stays up in May and June.

**Dr Frost** reviewers complain they "need to put everything in the order that they want for it to count" and that it "hates when you use 3.142 instead of pi" ([Trustpilot DFM](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/www.drfrostmaths.com)). The rule is lenient checking that accepts equivalent forms, and that shows the accepted forms on a wrong answer. Duolingo's data shows how much this matters: **"My answer should have been accepted" makes up about 72% of its roughly 200,000 daily reports** ([Duolingo ML triage](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-machine-learning-helps-duolingo-prioritize-course-improvements)). **Seneca** users describe being "bombarded by bloat whenever you finish a question", with distracting background animations and "silly gifs" that slow loading. Progress not saving is a top complaint, and previously free features have moved behind Premium ([Trustpilot Seneca](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/senecalearning.com?page=9); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.senecalearning.app&hl=en)). The rule is a calm completion state with no celebration screen between questions, and progress written to local storage on every answer. **Up Learn** is criticised as slow, repetitive and expensive. Its own counter-measure, quiz-check questions before videos so students can skip what they know, is the pattern to copy ([Trustpilot reply](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/uplearn.co.uk?page=4)).

The habit-loop evidence also says to avoid leaderboards. Duolingo learners who reach a **7-day streak are 3.6× more likely to finish their course** (correlational), and allowing two equipped streak freezes raised daily active learners by 0.38% ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). But a systematic mapping found that badges, leaderboards and competition are the gamification elements most often reported to have negative effects ([arXiv 2305.08346](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.08346)). Leagues also need a large user base, which a new CCEA-only app will not have. A private streak, an exam countdown and spec coverage give the same motivation without social pressure. For report flows, Duolingo users complain that the catch-all "Something else went wrong" option leaves them stuck and that reports disappear without reply ([Substack](https://mvanec.substack.com/p/duolingo-annoyances)). Close the loop with a "Your reports" list showing Received, Fixed, or Not an error with a reason, and publish a fix target the way Seneca does with "usually within one business day" ([Seneca help](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/2483276-where-can-i-report-a-mistake-in-the-content)). Duolingo found only about 10% of reports are real errors, so before a student submits a "wrong answer" report, show a deflection line: "Common mistake here: …".

## The AI tutor must be a hint ladder, not a chatbot

The case for restraint is now both empirical and regulatory. In Bastani et al.'s PNAS study of about 1,000 high-school maths students, unrestricted GPT access raised practice scores, but **cut later unassisted exam scores by 17%**. A guardrailed tutor that gave hints, not answers, largely removed the harm but did not produce a clear gain ([IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html); [Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education)). The DfE's Generative AI product safety standards, updated on 19 January 2026, expect learner-facing tools "not to provide final answers, full solutions, or complete worked examples by default". They also expect products to monitor cognitive offloading, avoid presenting the product as having its own agency, set time limits, detect distress and signpost human help, and avoid nudging users towards paid options. These summaries come from secondary sources and should be checked against GOV.UK before quoting ([Third Space Learning](https://thirdspacelearning.com/blog/dfe-generative-ai-product-safety-standards/); [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-ai-product-safety-standards)).

The competitors show which placement works. Up Learn's AI Tutor is grounded in its own content and tailored to the spec, warns that "it's always best to double-check key information", hands off to a human when the student types "speak to a human", and asks for a rating at the end ([Up Learn help](https://help.uplearn.co.uk/en/articles/13390598-what-is-the-ai-tutor)). Seneca turns chat off in teacher-set assignments ([Seneca Amelia](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/6992722-how-do-i-use-amelia-ai-assistant)). Khanmigo is Socratic, moderated, logged for parents and teachers, capped daily, and labelled as fallible ([Khan help](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/hu/articles/14394569357069)). Khanmigo's adult-alert moderation, however, depends on linked accounts, which an anonymous free app will not have. SME's Smart Mark skips calculation questions, and those make up most of CCEA Maths and Physics, so the competition offers nothing there. The version to build is an "Explain my mistake" button that appears only after a self-marked attempt and is scoped to that one question part. It should have a neutral name ("Hints", no avatar or human name), cite its source ("Based on: mark scheme Q4(b) M1"), apply a daily cap, and fall back to the authored walkthrough and the report flag. Leave AI out of the 12 December launch if moderation and distress signposting cannot be built properly by then. Ranks 1–10 deliver most of the value without it.

## An Apple-style direction that also behaves like Android

None of the competitor visual identities could be verified in this research. What is documented is the direction the leaders are moving in. Brilliant has simplified its course pages repeatedly, "easier to browse… less scrolling" in v5.0.0 and a "simplified Course page" in v7.6.0 ([APKMirror 5.0.0](https://www.apkmirror.com/apk/brilliant-org/brilliant/brilliant-5-0-0-release); [7.6.0](https://www.apkmirror.com/apk/brilliant-org/brilliant/brilliant-7-6-0-release)). The clearest visual complaints are about clutter, at Seneca and PMT. The multimedia research supports the same conclusion: removing seductive detail is among the largest effects in Mayer's corpus (overall g = 0.37) ([UCR meta-analysis](https://assess.ucr.edu/media/741/download)). An Apple-style direction is therefore a learning choice as well as a look.

Use these tokens. Make tap targets at least 44 px. That meets Apple's 44 pt guideline, though 48 px also matches Material's commonly cited 48 dp target, which was not verified in this research. Set body text at 17 px in rem units so Android font scaling works, and never go below 11 px ([HIG summary](https://skills.cat/skills/ehmo/platform-design-skills/ios-design-guidelines); verify against Apple's HIG). Use the font stack `-apple-system, system-ui, "Segoe UI", Roboto`, which resolves to Roboto on Android. Give each course a single accent colour (for example, one each for Maths, Further Maths and Physics) on a white or near-black surface. Show one question per screen with a large primary button pinned to the bottom. Use a bottom tab bar (Home, Practise, Papers, Progress). Open reports, hints and filters in bottom sheets. Apply translucent blur only to the tab bar, with a solid fallback, because `backdrop-filter` is costly on cheap phones. Use spring-eased transitions of 200–350 ms and turn them off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. The home screen holds a streak and daily-goal row, an exam countdown, a "Due today" queue, a "Next up" card and per-unit mastery rings.

The Android release adds requirements that Apple-style web design alone does not cover. Android 15 enforces edge-to-edge layout for apps targeting API 35, so use `viewport-fit=cover` and pad the header and tab bar with `env(safe-area-inset-*)` ([Android 15 UI changes](https://docs.airnativeextensions.com/news/2025/08/08/android-35-ui-changes); [Chromium cutout doc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src.git/+/fafff21/docs/ui/android/display_cutout.md)). Every sheet, step reveal and screen must push a history entry, so the system back gesture closes the sheet instead of exiting the app. Digital Asset Links must verify, or Chrome shows a URL bar. For performance, aim for good Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile (LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1) ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds)). Keep JavaScript well inside Alex Russell's global budget of about 300–350 KiB gzipped ([Infrequently Noted](https://infrequently.org/2022/12/)). Pre-render maths to HTML or SVG at build time and lazy-load the simulations and the AI. Reserve space for maths and fonts to prevent layout shift.

## Conclusion

The competitive picture points away from feature parity. The well-funded platforms compete on video, AI marking and dashboards for schools, and pay for them with paywalls, clutter and billing complaints. The free platforms compete on breadth and leave out progress tracking or worked solutions. A CCEA-only app run by one person wins by doing a narrower set of things properly: CCEA unit codes as navigation, every past-paper question tagged twice, mark-point self-marking, tap-through solutions, and progress that never disappears. AI is optional for this strategy. The strongest evidence in this research says that an answer-giving tutor makes students worse, and that a careful one at best breaks even.

The main open risks are verification gaps, not strategy. The notes could not confirm competitors' exact screens, whether Dr Frost hosts CCEA papers, how accurately students self-mark, or the final wording of the DfE standards. Before launch, spend an afternoon clicking through SME, Isaac and DFM on a cheap Android phone, and test self-marking with five CCEA students. Those two checks matter more than any further desk research.
